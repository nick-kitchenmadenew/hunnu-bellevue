## Kitchen Remodeling in Ballard

Ballard grew up around the ship canal and the Chittenden Locks, and much of its housing stock reflects that history — craftsman-style homes built well before an open-concept kitchen was the default expectation. Hunnu Construction has served the neighborhood since 2018, and a Ballard kitchen more often raises the layout question than a newer Eastside one does: whether the original, more closed-off footprint still works for how the house is actually lived in now, or whether the remodel is really an opportunity to open it up.

That's not a question with one right answer, and it isn't decided before the design consultation. Some Ballard homeowners want to preserve the house's original character and keep the kitchen's footprint close to what it always was, updating cabinets, counters, flooring and backsplash without touching the walls. Others are looking at a full reconfiguration. Both are legitimate starting points, and the honest conversation about which one fits happens in the home, not over the phone.

## Planning a Ballard Kitchen Project

A Ballard project starts with the same in-home design consultation as anywhere else in the service area — measuring the actual space, discussing what isn't working, and scoping the work to what the kitchen genuinely needs. An older home sometimes surfaces things a newer one wouldn't once work is underway — plumbing or electrical that predates current expectations, for instance — and that gets addressed as part of the project rather than treated as a surprise change order.

Once scope is set, the sequence stays consistent: cabinets confirmed first, countertops templated to them, flooring and backsplash finishing the room last, in the order that protects each surface from the ones installed after it. One crew carries a Ballard project through that whole sequence, the same as any other neighborhood Hunnu Construction works in.

## Cabinets and Backsplash in an Older Kitchen

Two trades come up more often in Ballard's older kitchens specifically: cabinets, because the originals were often built to a smaller scale than current cabinetry standard sizes, and backsplash, because a kitchen this age has frequently been painted or re-tiled at least once already, leaving layers worth checking before new tile goes up. Neither is a reason to avoid a standalone project — a cabinet installation or a backsplash update runs the same way it would in a newer kitchen, just with a wall or a cabinet cavity worth a closer look first.

A narrower original kitchen also means cabinet selection matters more to how the finished room actually feels — the same style of cabinet can read very differently at a scale built for a smaller footprint than at a scale built for an open, newer layout, which is part of what the design consultation works through before anything is ordered.

## Getting the Most From a Compact Layout

A smaller kitchen doesn't have room for wasted inches, and that shapes several decisions a larger Eastside kitchen doesn't have to make as carefully — cabinet depth near a doorway, how far a countertop can overhang before it crowds a walkway, whether a full-height backsplash makes a narrow room feel taller or simply busier. None of these are reasons to avoid updating a compact Ballard kitchen; they're the specific questions the design consultation is built to work through for a footprint like this one.

Storage tends to matter more in a smaller kitchen too, since there's less square footage to spread it across. Cabinet configuration — drawer versus door, how deep the uppers go, whether a corner cabinet actually gets used efficiently — often does more for how a compact kitchen functions day to day than any single finish choice.

Lighter cabinet and countertop colors also do real work in a smaller room with less natural light than a newer, more open layout gets — a choice that's more about how the finished kitchen actually feels to stand in every day than a stylistic preference, and worth discussing honestly at the design consultation rather than defaulting to whatever's currently trending in a showroom photo taken somewhere else entirely. A Ballard kitchen's own light and layout decide what actually works, not a trend.
