## Kitchen Remodeling in Kirkland

Kirkland runs along Lake Washington's east shore just north of Bellevue, and it's been part of Hunnu Construction's territory since 2018. The kitchens there range as widely as anywhere in the service area — from waterfront homes where a remodel is often about matching a higher-end finish package to the rest of the house, to inland streets where the more common project is a straightforward update to cabinets, counters or flooring that have simply reached the end of their life.

Whichever end of that range a project sits closer to, it starts the same way: an in-home design consultation to measure the actual kitchen and talk through what it needs, not an assumption based on the neighborhood or the size of the house. Material and finish choices — the specific cabinet line, the countertop material, the tile — get decided there, and the project is scoped and quoted around whatever's actually chosen.

## Planning a Kirkland Kitchen Project

A Kirkland project can be a single trade — a countertop, a set of cabinets, a floor — on its own, or a full renovation covering all four together, and neither gets presented as the default before the kitchen's actual condition is assessed. If the layout works and the cabinets are structurally sound, replacing only what's actually failed is usually the better fit on both cost and timeline than redoing the whole room.

The sequence is consistent regardless of scope: cabinets confirmed first, since counters get templated to them once they're in their final position, and flooring and backsplash finishing the room afterward. One crew carries that sequence through the whole project, from the first measurement to the final walkthrough, rather than assembling it from separately scheduled trades.

## What's Worth Flagging Early in a Kirkland Project

A home that's part of a homeowners' association, common in some of Kirkland's newer waterfront and planned communities, sometimes has its own rules about exterior changes, dumpster placement or contractor parking during a project. None of that changes the actual kitchen work, but flagging it during the design consultation means it's accounted for in the timeline rather than discovered as a delay once work has started.

The same goes for a home's specific plumbing or electrical setup if the project is a full renovation touching a sink or range location. Waterfront properties in particular can have infrastructure that's older or laid out differently than a standard suburban build, and that's exactly the kind of detail that gets surfaced during demolition if it wasn't already known — better raised early than found mid-project.

## Choosing Materials That Hold Up to Daily Use

Material choice matters more the more a kitchen actually gets used, and a higher-end finish package doesn't automatically mean a more durable one — some premium countertop and cabinet finishes are chosen for how they look rather than how they hold up to years of cooking, cleaning and the general wear a kitchen takes. Part of an honest design consultation is being direct about that tradeoff rather than only presenting the options that look best in a sample.

That conversation matters whether the project is a full renovation or a single trade like a countertop swap. A material that photographs well but scratches or stains easily is a choice a homeowner should make knowingly, not one that gets discovered as a problem a year into owning the finished kitchen.

The same honesty applies to cost. A higher-end material isn't automatically the right choice just because the home itself is higher-end, and a straightforward, durable option is sometimes the better fit for a kitchen that gets heavy daily use regardless of what the rest of the house looks like. That's a conversation worth having plainly rather than assuming the more expensive option is always the goal.

That plain-spoken approach carries through the whole project, not just the material conversation. A Kirkland homeowner gets the same honest scoping at the design consultation as anywhere else in the service area — what the kitchen actually needs, not what would make for the largest possible quote, waterfront address or not, standard suburban lot or otherwise. That's true whether the project is a full renovation or a single trade replaced on its own, and it doesn't change based on the neighborhood the house happens to sit in.
