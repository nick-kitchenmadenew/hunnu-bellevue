## Kitchen Remodeling in Magnolia

Magnolia sits on its own peninsula, bordered by Discovery Park on one side, and it's one of Seattle's quieter, more settled residential neighborhoods — the kind of place where a kitchen remodel is more often a long-considered decision than a reaction to something that just broke. Hunnu Construction has served the neighborhood since 2018, and the design consultation there is often as much about timing and planning as it is about scope, since many Magnolia projects aren't urgent.

That doesn't change what the project itself involves. A Magnolia kitchen gets measured and assessed the same way any kitchen does — what's working about the layout, what isn't, and whether one surface has failed while the rest of the room is sound. What can be different is the runway: a homeowner planning a project months out has more room to make unhurried decisions about cabinets, countertop material and layout than one dealing with a countertop that's actively failing.

## Planning a Magnolia Kitchen Project

A Magnolia project starts the same way any project does — an in-home design consultation to measure the actual kitchen and talk through what it needs. From there, the scope gets set honestly: a full renovation if the layout and more than one surface genuinely need to change, or a single trade — cabinets, countertop, flooring or backsplash — if that's all the kitchen actually requires.

The sequence stays the same regardless of how far in advance the project was planned: cabinets confirmed first, countertops templated once they're set, flooring and backsplash finishing the room last. One crew carries a Magnolia project through that sequence from the first measurement to the final walkthrough, whether the design consultation happened the week before demolition or months ahead of it.

## Planning Ahead Changes the Decisions, Not the Work

The advantage of a long runway isn't a different kind of project — it's more time to make each individual decision well. Choosing between countertop materials, working out a cabinet layout, deciding on tile for a backsplash: all of that benefits from not being rushed, and a homeowner who starts the design consultation months before they actually want work to begin has room to revisit choices before anything is ordered.

It also means material lead times stop being a scheduling pressure. Some cabinet lines and countertop materials take longer to arrive than others, and that's a non-issue for a project planned well ahead of when the homeowner actually wants the kitchen finished — it only becomes a constraint on a compressed timeline, which a Magnolia project doesn't have to be.

## A Quiet Neighborhood During Construction

Magnolia's residential streets are quieter than a lot of the service area, and that's worth a word about what construction actually looks like from the outside during a project — a dumpster staged in the driveway during demolition, deliveries of cabinets and material arriving over the course of the job, and the ordinary noise of a kitchen coming apart and going back together. None of it is unusual for the neighborhood, but a homeowner planning ahead sometimes wants to know what to expect before it starts.

That's a fair thing to raise at the design consultation, along with anything specific to the property — a shared driveway, limited street parking, a narrower access point than a truck might expect. Working those logistics out ahead of time is a small part of the planning conversation, but it's the kind of detail that keeps the first day of work from being the first time it comes up.

Neighbors are worth a heads-up too, particularly for a project that involves demolition — a short conversation ahead of time about which days will be loudest goes a long way on a quiet street, even though it isn't something Hunnu Construction manages directly. Knowing roughly what week demolition falls in, which the design consultation and quote both establish early, is enough for that conversation to happen well before anyone's asking about the dumpster parked in the driveway for a few days. A little advance notice tends to go a long way toward a smooth project on a street where everyone knows each other.
