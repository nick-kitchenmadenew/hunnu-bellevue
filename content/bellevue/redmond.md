## Kitchen Remodeling in Redmond

Redmond sits northeast of Bellevue, home to a large share of the region's tech workforce, and Hunnu Construction has served it as part of the Eastside area since 2018. A remodel there runs the same way it does anywhere else in the service area — an in-home design consultation, one crew across every trade the project needs, and one quote covering the whole job rather than a separate bill from every contractor involved.

Scheduling tends to matter more to Redmond households than the specifics of any one finish choice — a kitchen that's out of commission during a demolition and rough-in phase is a real disruption to a household running on a tight weekly routine, and that's exactly why the timeline gets laid out honestly during the design consultation rather than left as a moving target once work has already started.

## Planning a Redmond Kitchen Project

A Redmond project starts the same way every project does: measuring the actual kitchen, discussing what isn't working about it, and scoping the work honestly before anything is quoted. Whether that turns out to be a full renovation — layout, cabinets, counters, floor and backsplash together — or a single trade replaced on its own depends entirely on what the kitchen actually needs, not on which is the bigger job to sell.

Once the scope is set, the sequence is the same one every kitchen in the service area follows — cabinets first, since counters get templated to them and everything else in the room is measured against their final position; flooring and backsplash finishing the job once the surfaces above and around them are set. One crew carries that sequence from start to finish, which is what keeps a tight household schedule from being at the mercy of four different contractors' four different calendars.

## Why One Point of Contact Matters More on a Tight Schedule

A household juggling two working schedules doesn't have much room for a project that drags because one contractor's delay pushed back the next one's start date — and that's the exact failure mode a single crew across every trade is built to avoid. When the same team handles cabinets, counters, flooring and backsplash, there's one calendar to manage instead of four, and one person to ask when something needs to move rather than four separate calls to make.

That matters even on a smaller, single-trade project. A countertop replacement that's supposed to take a few days doesn't need four separate scheduling conversations, and a household that can only realistically be without a kitchen for a certain window benefits from a quote and a timeline set once, honestly, rather than revised as the project goes.

## Design Decisions That Don't Need to Take Long

A busy household doesn't have to mean rushed decisions about cabinets, countertop material or layout — it means the process around those decisions has to respect the time available. The design consultation is scoped to get through the real questions efficiently: what's not working about the current kitchen, what the realistic options are, and what a given choice actually costs and takes, without dragging the conversation out over multiple visits before anything is decided.

The goal of that first visit is a scope and a quote that can be reviewed and decided on in the time a household actually has, not a drawn-out back-and-forth spread across multiple visits that adds weeks before any work even starts. Material and finish questions get answered as directly as the kitchen itself allows.

Once a project starts, the same principle applies to updates along the way — a household that can't be reached during business hours every day still gets a clear sense of where the project stands, rather than being left to wonder whether a delay in one part of the job means the whole timeline has shifted. A quick update at the end of a workday is enough to keep a busy Redmond household in the loop without adding another meeting to an already full calendar, and it's offered as a matter of course rather than something that has to be requested.
