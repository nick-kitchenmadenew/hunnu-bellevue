## Kitchen Remodeling in Sammamish

Sammamish sits east of Bellevue and Issaquah, largely built out more recently than some of the closer-in Eastside cities, and it's been part of Hunnu Construction's service area since 2018. A newer subdivision generally means a kitchen with a layout that already works — the more common project there is an upgrade to one or two things that were chosen to a builder's budget rather than a homeowner's taste, not a full reconfiguration of the room.

That doesn't mean every Sammamish project is small. A house that's a decade or more old has cabinets, counters and flooring that have had a decade of daily use, and any of those can genuinely need replacing regardless of how well the layout itself has held up. What the design consultation actually decides is which situation applies — a single-trade upgrade, or something closer to a full renovation — rather than assuming based on the age of the house alone.

## Planning a Sammamish Kitchen Project

Every Sammamish project starts with the same design consultation as anywhere else in the service area — an in-home visit to measure the kitchen and talk through what's actually not working, whether that's one surface or the whole layout. From there, the scope is set honestly: a cabinet or countertop swap on its own if that's genuinely all the kitchen needs, or a full renovation if more than one part of it has reached the end of its life at the same time.

The sequence stays the same regardless of scope — cabinets set and confirmed first, countertops templated to them once they're in place, flooring and backsplash finishing the room last. One crew carries a Sammamish project through that whole sequence, the same as it does anywhere else Hunnu Construction works.

## Kitchens Built for a Household, Not Just a Floor Plan

Many Sammamish homes were built with larger kitchens to begin with — bigger islands, more counter run, more cabinet storage than an older, smaller Eastside kitchen would have. That extra square footage changes the math on a remodel more than it might seem: more counter means more material for a countertop replacement, more cabinet runs means more boxes to level and set for a cabinet installation, and a larger backsplash area to tile.

None of that changes the sequence a project follows, but it does change the honest scope and timeline a design consultation lands on. A larger kitchen isn't automatically a bigger, more complicated project — often it's the same straightforward trade, just more of it — and that's exactly the kind of distinction that gets sorted out by actually measuring the room rather than guessing from square footage alone.

## Kitchens Built for Entertaining

A larger island and more open sightlines to the rest of the house are common in Sammamish's newer construction, and they change how a remodel gets approached more than the square footage alone does. An island that also functions as seating, a prep area visible from where guests gather, and enough counter run to actually host a group all put more weight on the layout decisions made during the design consultation than they would in a smaller, more closed-off kitchen.

That's part of why measuring the actual room matters even more here — a countertop or cabinet run sized for a smaller kitchen doesn't translate cleanly to a larger island-centered layout, and getting the proportions right is what separates an island that reads as the room's centerpiece from one that reads as oversized or awkwardly placed.

Seating at an island raises its own set of questions too — overhang depth for knees, spacing between stools, whether the island needs additional support to carry the extra overhang safely. None of those are decided by guesswork; they're worked out against the actual island dimensions during the design consultation, before the countertop is ever templated, so the finished island actually seats people comfortably rather than just looking like it should from a distance. A larger kitchen deserves the same care in the details as a smaller one, not just more square footage to work with.
