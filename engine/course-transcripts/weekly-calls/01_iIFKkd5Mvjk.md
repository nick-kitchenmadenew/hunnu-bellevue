## Main Content

The call focused on shifting SEO strategies away from traditional keyword volume research toward a "rank mapping" approach, alongside practical advice on agency operations, content production, and website structure.

**Rank Mapping vs. Keyword Research**
*   **Keyword Planner Myth:** Google Keyword Planner is designed for paid advertising, not organic SEO. Its search volume data is often misleading or inaccurate for local SEO.
*   **Rank Mapping Strategy:** Agencies should prioritize content creation based on rank map results rather than search volume. If a primary category ranks well, the site will likely rank for related services. If a specific service is a priority, run a separate rank map for it.
*   **Goal:** The ultimate objective is to achieve "top three" rankings across all target keywords and locations.

**Website & Content Structure**
*   **Mimic the GBP:** Website structure should mirror the Google Business Profile (GBP) structure. This strengthens topical authority.
*   **Content Tiering:**
    *   **Homepage:** Focus on the primary category, city, and goal completion.
    *   **Sub-pages:** Link primary categories to secondary category pages. 
    *   **Services:** Link high-value services directly from the homepage if they are critical for client revenue, even if it disrupts standard folder hierarchies.
*   **Topical Relevance:** Use FAQs and detailed articles (answering specific questions found on Reddit or Google’s "People Also Ask") to build site authority.

**Agency Operations & Team Management**
*   **Writer Hiring:** Avoid hiring copywriters with prior "SEO baggage" (knowledge), as they often resist specific workflows. Train them from scratch.
*   **Payment Model:** Do not pay writers hourly, as it creates poor incentives. Pay per article ($15–$30 depending on experience/volume).
*   **Workflow:** Use a simple Google Sheet to track content briefs, status, and links rather than complex project management software (like Asana), which can create notification fatigue for the team.
*   **Quality Assurance:** Use a "content brief" system where writers produce content, a manager checks the schema and links, and AI is used for quality control (e.g., zero GPT checks) before publishing.
*   **Images:** Avoid stock photos. Use client-provided images or AI-generated images that maintain consistency (logos, uniforms).
