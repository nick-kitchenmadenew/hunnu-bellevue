## Main Content

This session served as a strategic Q&A for SEO professionals, focusing on the paradigm shift toward "AI-first" optimization. The primary discussion centered on how businesses can manipulate ChatGPT and Google's AI Overview to secure recommendations, moving away from traditional backlink-heavy strategies.

**Key strategic areas discussed include:**

*   **AI and Schema Markup:** The host emphasized that AI models (like ChatGPT) rely heavily on Schema markup to understand business entities and services. Participants are advised to use "nested" Schema (e.g., placing service schema inside local business schema) to create clear relationships that the AI can easily parse and recommend.
*   **Citation Consistency:** Unlike traditional SEO where citations were often dismissed, the host argued they are now critical for AI recommendations. A single discrepancy in a business name or address across platforms can disqualify a business from being recommended by AI tools.
*   **Website Architecture & Redesigns:** The group discussed the dangers of website redesigns. Launching a new site—even on an existing domain—often results in a ranking drop requiring a 3-6 month recovery period. The host noted that Google determines site architecture through internal linking, not URL folder structure, so URLs should be kept simple.
*   **Geographic Ranking:** Attempting to rank in cities outside a business’s physical location is high-effort and often futile due to proximity algorithms. The recommended strategy is to focus on hyper-local neighborhood content within the primary city.
*   **Client Acquisition Strategy:** Cold email is becoming increasingly difficult due to stringent spam filters and the difficulty of getting media (GIFs/images) to display correctly. The host recommends shifting toward low-ticket offers (e.g., website builds at $300 or monthly GBP management) and using Facebook/YouTube ads to scale, as this avoids the reliance on high-friction sales calls and complex email outreach.
*   **Internal Linking:** Sites should be siloed by topic. Cross-linking between service categories confuses the algorithm and dilutes topical relevance.
